Music, Mood & Working Memory

This study looks at how self-selected music relates to performance on a short working-memory task in high-school students.

Before you begin

Please use a laptop or desktop with a physical keyboard, have headphones or earbuds available, and choose a quiet place where you are unlikely to be interrupted for about 20–25 minutes.

Study information & assent

You are invited to take part in an independent student research study about music, mood, anxiety, and working memory. Participation is voluntary. You may stop at any time without penalty.

You will answer short questions about your recent mood and anxiety, your music-listening habits, and basic demographics. You will also complete two short 2-back working-memory blocks: one while listening to self-selected music and one in silence.

The questionnaires are research measures, not diagnoses. Your individual depression or anxiety scores will not be shown to you. Research responses are stored without your name, email address, exact birth date, student ID, or school name.

Some questions ask about sadness, worry, sleep, concentration, and energy. If a question makes you uncomfortable, you may stop the study. If you would like support, talk with a parent/guardian, school counselor, healthcare professional, or another trusted adult.

Research contact: Esha Swamy (eshaswamy@icloud.com)

Data and withdrawal: You may stop at any time before the final submission without penalty. Your research answers are sent only when you select Submit study at the end. The research dataset does not include your name, email, school name, exact birth date, or student ID. Because submitted responses are anonymous and are not linked to your parent/guardian permission record, a specific research response generally cannot be identified and removed after submission. Results will be reported only in summary or aggregate form. Research data will be stored in a password-protected Google account and deleted no later than one year after the project is completed.
