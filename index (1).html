Music, Well-Being & Memory

Does music help you focus, or make it harder? Try two short memory challenges to help us explore the answer.

Before you begin

What are we trying to find out?

This research study examines how listening to self-selected music, compared with silence, relates to working-memory performance in high-school students. It also explores whether individual differences in emotional well-being, music-listening habits, and reasons for listening to music are associated with working-memory performance.

What will I be doing?

If you participate, you will:

  • Answer basic questions about yourself, such as your age, grade, typical sleep, music experience, and study-music habits.
  • Answer short questions about your emotional well-being over the past two weeks, including your mood, energy, and everyday experiences.
  • Answer questions about why you listen to music.
  • Choose music that you enjoy and might normally listen to while doing something that requires concentration.
  • Complete a short practice working-memory task.
  • Try two short letter-memory challenges: one while listening to music you choose and one in silence.
  • Answer a few questions about your experience during the tasks.

The study should take approximately 10–15 minutes. Actual time may vary.

Is there anything I should know?

This study involves minimal anticipated risk. Some questionnaire items ask about mood, worry, sleep, concentration, and energy and may feel personal or uncomfortable. You can stop before submitting if any question makes you uncomfortable. You can also talk with a trusted adult if you have questions or concerns.

How will my participation help?

You may not personally benefit from participating. The results may help researchers better understand how self-selected music relates to concentration and working-memory performance in adolescents and why music may affect different students differently.

Do I have to participate?

Participation is completely voluntary. You may stop at any point before submitting your responses without penalty.

What information will be collected?

The study asks about age, grade, country, sleep, music experience, and letter-memory performance. The emotional well-being questions include standardized measures commonly used in research on depression and anxiety. They do not assume you have either condition and are not medical tests or diagnoses. All questionnaire items are required to proceed; you may stop at any time before submission.

We do not ask for your name, email, school, or exact birth date. Your answers are stored using a randomly generated study ID in a password-protected Google account and reported only in summary form. The hosting and submission services may process technical connection information. After anonymous submission, we generally cannot identify and remove an individual response. Study data will be deleted within one year after the project ends.
