Music, Mental Health & Working Memory

Informed Consent

What is this study about?

This research study examines how listening to self-selected music, compared with silence, relates to working-memory performance in high-school students. It also examines whether differences in depression and anxiety symptoms, music-listening habits, and reasons for listening to music are associated with differences in how students perform with music.

What will I do?

If you participate, you will:

  • Answer basic questions about yourself, such as your age, grade, typical sleep, music experience, and study-music habits.
  • Answer short questionnaires about your recent depression and anxiety symptoms.
  • Answer questions about why you listen to music.
  • Choose music that you enjoy and might normally listen to while doing something that requires concentration.
  • Complete a short practice working-memory task.
  • Complete two 2-back working-memory blocks: one while listening to your self-selected music and one in silence.
  • Answer a few questions about your experience during the tasks.

The study should take approximately 10–15 minutes. Actual time may vary.

Are there any risks or discomforts?

This study involves minimal anticipated risk. Some questionnaire items ask about mood, worry, sleep, concentration, and energy and may feel personal or uncomfortable. If something in the study makes you uncomfortable or you would like support, consider talking with a healthcare professional or trusted adult.

Are there any benefits?

You may not personally benefit from participating. The results may help researchers better understand how self-selected music relates to concentration and working-memory performance in adolescents and why music may affect different students differently.

Is participation required?

Participation is completely voluntary. You may stop at any point before submitting your responses without penalty.

What information will be collected?

The study collects information including demographics (i.e. age and grade), music experience, mental health, and working memory. The mood and anxiety questionnaires in this study are being used for research purposes only. They do not provide a medical diagnosis.

This survey is anonymous and does not ask for your personal information. Research responses will be stored in a password-protected Google account.

2-back practice

Letters will appear one at a time. Press the SPACE BAR whenever the current letter is the same as the letter shown two letters earlier. If it does not match, do not press anything.

ABA
